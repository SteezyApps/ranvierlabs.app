How it works

  1. 1

    Change a variable

    Pick a scenario, disease or drug and set how strong it is: 30% blood loss, a beta-blocker, a hot day.

  2. 2

    Watch the body respond, live

    Heart rate, blood pressure, cardiac output and stroke volume move together on live traces. Speed time up from seconds to hours.

  3. 3

    Run real scenarios

    Work through patient cases: predict which way each value will move, run it, then see what you got right and why.

Run a scenario in one tap

A few of the scenarios built in. Pick one, set the severity, and watch every system respond.

Hemorrhage

Lose 30% of blood volume and watch the heart speed up to hold pressure as stroke volume falls.

Standing up fast

Go from lying down to upright and see the baroreflex catch the drop in blood pressure.

Exercise

Push to 70% of max effort and watch heart rate and cardiac output climb together.

Dehydration

Lose fluid over hours and watch blood pressure, stroke volume and the kidneys respond.

Made by a physiology instructor

Homeostasis is built by a university physiology instructor who teaches these systems to nursing and pre-health students, and designed around the concepts students find hardest to picture.

Questions

Is it on Android?

Not yet. Homeostasis is coming to iPhone first.

Does it replace my review course?

No. It makes the concepts click, so the questions in your review course make sense.

Who’s it for?

Nursing students, from your first pathophysiology course to NCLEX prep. It also works for anyone taking physiology, PA or med school courses.

Is it a clinical tool?

It is not a clinical tool. The scenarios, diseases and drug doses in it exist to teach mechanisms. Do not use it to make decisions about a real patient, and do not use it for diagnosis, treatment or dosing.
